Health insurers sell off on CMS Medicare Advantage regulatory proposals (RADV audit extrapolation and star-ratings overhaul) plus renewed political breakup/Medicare-for-All pressure

CMS proposes CRUSH anti-fraud rule extrapolating Medicare Advantage RADV audit findings across whole contracts retroactive to payment year 2018, followed days later by a star-ratings overhaul estimated to cut ~$107B of quality bonus payments over a decade -> investors mark down future MA revenue/margins and add a contingent liability for prior-year clawbacks -> multiple compression concentrated in the most MA-exposed names (UNH, HUM, CVS), amplified by Democratic congressional sweep reviving Medicare-for-All and Warren's conglomerate-breakup push -> broad, correlated 5-day industry decline

The decline is well explained by two government actions in the same week: the CMS CRUSH proposal applying RADV audit extrapolation to entire MA contracts back to payment year 2018 [5][13][22][49], and a separate star-ratings overhaul CMS estimates would cut MA bonus payments by roughly $107B between 2031 and 2037 [58][59][60][67]. Dispersion matches MA exposure: Humana fell hardest on the audit news given MA is the bulk of its revenue [22][26], while Cigna, which exited MA and is now mostly Evernorth, is the best performer [35][28] — consistent with a regulatory rather than macro cause, given the S&P fell only 2.4%. Political overhang compounded it: a Democratic congressional sweep revived Medicare for All and Warren called for breaking up UNH, CVS and Cigna [4][21][63]. Offsetting evidence argues against permanence: both items are proposals subject to comment and likely litigation (UNH is already suing CMS over star ratings [51]), recent operating results were strong with ELV raising 2026 EPS guidance and reiterating 12%+ 2027 growth [49] and Centene beating in Q3 [39], and TD Cowen upgraded UNH and HUM mid-selloff on improving MA pricing [34][36][42]. The star-ratings cash impact is dated 2031-2037, so near-term earnings damage is more about the retroactive RADV clawback risk, which Morgan Stanley reportedly sized at a very wide 11%-51% of 2028 EPS — an honest signal that magnitude is unresolved.
